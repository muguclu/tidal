# Cost-optimisation report (T-507)

_Generated by `docs/finops/cost_optimization_report.py` from `docs/finops/prices.yaml` (Azure Retail Prices API, westeurope, EUR, captured 2026-10-24) and the committed model scripts. Do not edit by hand; re-run the script. Labels: **MEASURED** = counted by a run (local stack only so far), **MODELLED** = unit price x measured or stated quantity, **NEEDS-CLOUD** = needs a deployed environment and a real bill. Nothing in this repository has been deployed to Azure yet, so no EUR figure below is an invoice._

## Executive summary (for the trading manager)

* **What it costs.** A month with about 40 hours of real use is modelled at EUR 68.40 to 126.86 with the database at list price (EUR 54.44 to 82.61 on the free database offer). Parked outside those hours it is EUR 46.61 (EUR 42.76 on the free offer). The parked floor is mostly the Kubernetes load balancer, address and disk (EUR 24.38), the web plan (EUR 11.53) and the container registry (EUR 4.40). MODELLED.
* **Biggest levers are compute that is switched off, not clever storage.** Backtest nodes on cheap interruptible machines that exist only while a sweep runs: EUR 667.95 to EUR 1.63-6.12 per month (99.1 %-99.8 % less). A database that pauses when idle: EUR 218.91 to EUR 13.95-44.25. Parking the environment: EUR 66.72 to EUR 36.95-24.38. All MODELLED.
* **Storing the data smaller is real but worth cents.** The epoch Parquet format is 3.6x smaller than the relational baseline (MEASURED bytes), EUR 0.2581 versus EUR 0.0101 per month at the 50 M-tick scale (MODELLED). Moving old rows to cheaper blob tiers nets EUR 0.0100 per month at the 300 M-tick scale; it is a design-correctness point, not a budget lever.
* **Measured vs modelled.** MEASURED: file sizes, the local SQL-to-blob migration (SQL space 137,040 KB to 34,960 KB reserved), and local request latency (median 91 ms over 89 requests, a warm laptop reference). Everything in EUR is MODELLED from list prices and stated usage assumptions, and the savings depend on those assumptions (for example the serverless database stops saving money above 426 active hours a month).
* **What still needs a cloud run.** All of C4 to C10 and the Azure-side parts of C1 to C3: real node-hours per 100 backtests, cold-start and database-resume delay (the price of scale-to-zero), the real log volume, and the actual bill. The checklist is at the end of this report; one week of the dev environment is enough to turn most rows from MODELLED into MEASURED.

## C1. Epoch time-series format

Method: the T-204 benchmark stores the same synthetic ticks in seven formats; bytes at rest are counted, then priced at SQL GP storage (0.1204 EUR/GiB-month) versus Blob Hot (0.0172). F5 is 3.6x smaller on the 3-month slice (MEASURED) and 2.144 -> 0.589 GiB at full S (F0 MODELLED by bytes/tick, F5 MEASURED).

Evidence: [c1-c3.md](results/c1-c3.md), [storage-benchmark-2026-10-22.md](results/storage-benchmark-2026-10-22.md). Caveat: F0 was only run on the 3-month slice; the full-S F0 figure assumes linear row width. Absolute amounts are cents at every scale.

## C2. SQL to object storage migration

Method: MEASURED on the local stack, `tidal tiering run` moved 2,923,720 tick rows and 1,218,173 bar rows to Parquet; the tick table went from 71,368 to 2,184 KB reserved, 25.00 B/tick in SQL versus 12.60 B/tick as Parquet. The EUR figure is MODELLED: always-on provisioned 2 vCore versus serverless 1 vCore average for 176 h, 90 days hot, and 100,000 cold queries a month. S: 215.20 -> 89.83; M: 215.90 -> 89.89; L: 219.26 -> 90.16.

Evidence: [c1-c3.md](results/c1-c3.md), `results/c2/`. Caveat: the saving is SQL compute, which depends on the duty cycle; it vanishes at 426 active hours a month at 1 vCore. The provisioned meter may exclude licence, so the baseline is a lower bound.

## C3. Blob lifecycle tiering by data age

Method: MODELLED by `deltas/T-207_model.py` (ADR-0015: ticks only, two index tags). Net versus all-Hot: S +0.0010, M +0.0100, L +0.1108 EUR/month, after index-tag cost and compaction reads. With the earlier five-tag design the same model gives S -0.0008, M -0.0023, L +0.0861, which is the tag-cost finding in the trade-offs below.

Evidence: [c1-c3.md](results/c1-c3.md), `deltas/T-207.md`. Caveat: tier capacity (`BlobCapacity` by `Tier`) and the lifecycle engine need a real account; Azurite has neither.

## C4. Dynamic autoscaling (AKS spot, KEDA 0..N)

Method: MODELLED, baseline 5 x D4as_v5 on demand x 730 h = 667.95; optimised 40 / 150 spot node-hours (assumed) at 0.0338 EUR/h plus a prorated 32 GB P4 disk = 1.63 / 6.12; idle cost of the pool is 0 at zero nodes. Spot/on-demand price ratio 0.185.

Evidence: [c4-c10.md](results/c4-c10.md) (method for the cloud measurement of node-hours by pool and EUR per 100 backtests). Caveat: the node-hours are assumptions; EUR per 100 backtests is not modelled at all.

## C5. Serverless containers

Method: MODELLED, B2 always on 730 h x 0.0317 = 23.14 versus Container Apps with 90,000 of 180,000 free vCPU-seconds and 180,000 of 360,000 free GiB-seconds used. Compute meters round to 0.0 in the retail API, so the true saving is read from the cost export. Local warm reference (MEASURED, laptop): median 91 ms, p95 1100 ms over 89 requests; that p95 is warm-up noise, not a cold start.

Evidence: [c4-c10.md](results/c4-c10.md). Caveat: cold-start p95 is the cost of this saving and is NEEDS-CLOUD.

## C6. SQL serverless auto-pause

Method: MODELLED, provisioned 2 vCore = 218.91 (lower bound) versus serverless 40 active hours at 0.5 vCore 13.95 or 2 vCore 44.25; paused all month = 3.85 (storage). Dev uses the free offer (ADR-0011), about 0, until its allowance is used.

Evidence: [c4-c10.md](results/c4-c10.md). Caveat: resume latency and the 60-minute idle tail billed at the active rate are NEEDS-CLOUD.

## C7. Scheduled VM

Method: MODELLED, 730 h x 0.0106 + 2.1119 disk = 9.85 versus 5.0 h/month + disk = 2.16. The disk bills while deallocated, so the floor is 2.11.

Evidence: [c4-c10.md](results/c4-c10.md). Caveat: running hours come from the activity log in the cloud window.

## C8. Log ingestion control

Method: MODELLED, ceiling 0.2 GB/day x 30 = 6.0 GB/month, first 5 GB free, rest 2.6311 EUR/GB = 2.63. The uncapped baselines are assumed rates, not observations. A Basic Logs table would cost 3.43 for the same volume (no free tier), so it is not used for the capped path. The T-506 alert rules add 0.26 EUR/month inside this budget.

Evidence: [c4-c10.md](results/c4-c10.md), `deltas/T-120.md`, `deltas/T-506.md`. Caveat: when the cap is hit ingestion stops for the day.

## C9. ACR hygiene

Method: MODELLED, assumed 40 images/month x 0.25 GB, 10 GB included, 0.088 EUR/GB-month above. Without purge the bill grows to 4.40 (month 6) and 9.68 (month 12); with weekly purge it stays at the included amount.

Evidence: [c4-c10.md](results/c4-c10.md), `deltas/T-122.md`. Caveat: small in EUR; the point is unbounded growth. Image size and cadence are assumptions.

## C10. Parking

Method: MODELLED from the stoppable lines (AKS system B2s, App Service B1) and the retained ones (disk, LB, IP): always on 66.72; parked outside work hours (217 h of 730) 36.95; fully parked 24.38. If Azure releases the LB and IP when the cluster stops the parked floor falls to the disk alone (5.11); this is UNVERIFIED (T-306, T-313).

Evidence: [c4-c10.md](results/c4-c10.md), `runbooks/park.md`. Caveat: the export is daily, so work-hour parking within a day is not visible and stays MODELLED.

## Trade-offs (SPEC-09 section 4), next to the saving

* **C5 cold start.** Scale-to-zero saves 23.14/month and costs the first request after idle: replica start, image pull and DuckDB Azure extension load. Cloud p95 is NEEDS-CLOUD. Lever: `min_replicas = 1`. Not for production if a trader waits on the first screen of the day.
* **C6 resume latency.** Auto-pause saves up to 215.06/month; the first connection after a pause waits tens of seconds (SPEC-09 says about a minute) or fails, and up to 60 min after the last connection bills at the active rate. Production would keep a floor of provisioned or longer pause delay.
* **C4 spot evictions.** Spot is 18.5% of the on-demand price. A node can be evicted at any time; the job is redelivered from Service Bus and rerun, so a backtest can take longer and a sweep has a tail. Safe because backtests are deterministic and idempotent. Not for latency-bound work.
* **C3 tiering retrieval and early delete.** Cool and Cold charge per GB retrieved (0.0088 / 0.0264 EUR/GB) and per 10 K operations; compaction of a tiered partition pays them again; Cold has a 90-day minimum (0.004 EUR/GB early-delete). Archive is excluded for market data (rehydration time, ADR-0004). The net gain is EUR 0.1108/month even at L.
* **Tag-cost finding (ADR-0015).** Index tags bill per tag per month in every tier (0.0343 EUR per 10 K). Five tags on every part made tiering a net loss at S and M (-0.0008 and -0.0023). Tagging ticks only with two tags turns it positive (+0.0010 and +0.0100). Cost: bars are never tiered.
* **Private networking.** The optional `enable_private_networking` flag adds EUR 24.95/month active (EUR 53.86 with a NAT gateway) for private endpoints, DNS zones and the VM outbound address. It buys removal of public data-plane endpoints (SPEC-06) and costs several times the whole parked bill, so it is off in dev.
* **Defender for SQL off.** Enabling it adds EUR 13.20/month per server (ADR-0017) and raises the dev log ceiling. Off by default: vulnerability assessment and threat alerts are not running. Production would turn it on.
* **C2 and C1 file format.** Queries older than 90 days go through DuckDB over Blob (higher first-byte latency, per-query transaction cost); point lookups are slower than a clustered index (T-204 W4); restatements are new versions (ADR-0014); the tiering job is one more moving part.
* **C10 parking.** Unpark takes minutes to a deploy cycle, jobs do not run while parked, and `terraform destroy` remains the real control for long gaps.

## What would change the conclusions

* Serverless active hours above 426 a month (C2/C6), or a provisioned meter that includes licence (baseline rises, saving rises).
* Real log volume far below the assumed uncapped rates (C8 saving shrinks) or above the cap (diagnostics lost).
* Spot capacity or eviction rate that stretches sweeps (C4), or `az aks stop` releasing the LB and IP (parked floor falls).
* Larger data: at production scale (reservations, savings plans, Hybrid Benefit, dev/test pricing, FinOps governance) the compute rows dominate even more; storage rows stay small until well past L scale.
