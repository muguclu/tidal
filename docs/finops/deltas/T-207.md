# Cost delta T-207 (storage lifecycle policy, `modules/storage/lifecycle.tf`)

Status: MODELLED. Unit prices from `docs/finops/prices.yaml` (captured 2026-10-22, EUR, westeurope), including the new
`blob_index_tags_10k_month` meter added in this PR through `tidal.finops.fetch_prices` (Azure Retail Prices API,
"Index Tags", Blob Storage, 0.0343 EUR per 10K tags per month). No measurement yet; T-503 replaces these with Cost
Management numbers. The policy itself has no charge; the costs are tag storage, tier-change operations, compaction reads
and early deletion.

## Rules

| Rule | Filter | Action |
|---|---|---|
| md-cool | `marketdata/v1/`, blockBlob, tags `age_band=cool` AND `compacted=true` | Cool, 0 days since modification |
| md-cold | `marketdata/v1/`, blockBlob, tags `age_band=cold` AND `compacted=true` | Cold, 0 days |
| raw | `raw/` | Cool @ 30 d, Archive @ 180 d |
| backtests | `backtests/` | Cool @ 30 d, delete @ 365 d |
| exports | `exports/` | delete @ 14 d |
| bench | `bench/` | delete @ 30 d |

`age_band=hot` has no rule: hot data stays in Hot. Tag filters are AND-ed, support only `==`, and never match an untagged
blob, so the derived `_manifest.json` (not tagged by `retag`) can never be tiered. Market data never reaches Archive
(ADR-0004). `raw/` does: replays of `raw/` data older than 180 days need rehydration (hours of latency plus the Archive
retrieval and read meters); acceptable for replay/audit only.

SPEC-03 section 4 now carries the `compacted=true` guard (see "Compaction guard" there). It avoids tiering parts that
yearly compaction is about to delete.

## Volume model (stated, not measured)

Bytes as in T-121: 10 bytes/row, S = 0.5 GB, M = 3 GB, L = 15 GB; split 15 % hot / 35 % cool / 50 % cold by data age.
Parts (storage-layout section 2; scales from config-contract: S 3 instruments x 5 y, M 10 x 10 y, L 10 x 20 y):
monthly parts for `ticks`, `bars_1s`, `bars_1m`, `bars_1h` (instruments x years x 12), yearly parts for `bars_1d`
(instruments x years); one part per partition after compaction.

| Scale | Bytes (GB) | Parts, ticks only | Parts, all five datasets |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | 0.5 | 180 | 735 |
| M | 3 | 1,200 | 4,900 |
| L | 15 | 2,400 | 9,800 |

Compaction and retag run for all five datasets (SPEC-03 section 3.1), so "all five datasets" is the planning case. The
extra datasets add few bytes, so bytes stay as in T-121 (conservative for tag cost per byte). Each part carries 5 tags
(`dataset`, `instrument`, `data_year`, `age_band`, `compacted`); tags are billed on every part in every tier, hot ones too.
The transient doubling of part count during compaction is ignored.

## Tag storage (new)

Cost = parts x tags per part / 10,000 x 0.0343 EUR per month.

| Scale | Tags (5/part) | Tag cost EUR/month | Tiering saving vs all-Hot EUR/month | Net after tag cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 3,675 | 0.0126 | 0.0048 (0.0086 -> 0.0038) | -0.0078 |
| M | 24,500 | 0.0840 | 0.0286 (0.0516 -> 0.0230) | -0.0554 |
| L | 49,000 | 0.1681 | 0.1431 (0.2580 -> 0.1149) | -0.0250 |

## Tier-change one-off (recomputed)

Split of parts by band (35 % to Cool, 50 % to Cold, 15 % stay Hot), moved from Hot by the lifecycle rules:
ops = cool parts x 0.088 EUR/10K (`blob_cool_write_10k`) + cold parts x 0.1584 EUR/10K (`blob_cold_write_10k`); data write
into Cool 0.0022 EUR/GB on 35 % of the bytes. `prices.yaml` has no per-GB Cold write meter, so Cold data write is unpriced here.

| Scale (all-five parts) | Cool parts | Cold parts | Ops EUR | Cool data write EUR | One-off EUR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 257 | 368 | 0.0081 | 0.0004 | 0.0085 |
| M | 1,715 | 2,450 | 0.0539 | 0.0023 | 0.0562 |
| L | 3,430 | 4,900 | 0.1078 | 0.0116 | 0.1194 |

The earlier 0.50 EUR at L came from an unsupported 45,000-part assumption; with the 9,800 parts derived above it is 0.12
EUR (0.50 EUR is the figure at 45K parts, kept as a sensitivity). Later steps: each year the partitions crossing the
3-year and 10-year boundaries move Hot -> Cool and Cool -> Cold (a Cold write op each). At steady state that is
parts / years per boundary: L 490 parts per boundary, 0.0121 EUR/year for both ops (S 0.0036, M 0.0121). Cool -> Cold
takes place a year or more after the Cool move, so no Cool early-deletion fee applies.

## Yearly compaction read cost (new)

`compact_partition` reads every part of every partition (even a single compacted part is read and digested), including
parts in Cool and Cold. Per year: retrieval 0.0088 EUR/GB (Cool) and 0.0264 EUR/GB (Cold) on 35 % / 50 % of the bytes, plus
read ops 0.0088 (Cool) and 0.088 (Cold) EUR per 10K.

| Scale | Retrieval EUR/year | Read ops EUR/year | Total EUR/year | EUR/month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 0.0081 | 0.0035 | 0.0116 | 0.0010 |
| M | 0.0488 | 0.0231 | 0.0719 | 0.0060 |
| L | 0.2442 | 0.0461 | 0.2903 | 0.0242 |

This is a finding for T-205: skipping partitions that are already a single `compacted=true` part would remove almost all
of this cost (Cold/Cool reads would only be paid for partitions that received new parts). Not changed here.

## Early-deletion interaction

Early deletion is billed on the time since the move into the tier, not since creation. Cold has an explicit meter
(`blob_cold_early_delete_gb`; the unit is per GB-month of unmet retention at 0.004 EUR, 90-day minimum, so at most
3 x 0.004 = 0.012 EUR/GB). Cool has no early-delete meter in the API; the Cool fee is **derived**:
`blob_cool_stored_gb_month` x remaining days / 30 (at most 0.0088 EUR/GB). Without the `compacted=true` guard the worst case
is every part tiered then deleted by compaction: those fees plus the tier-change write. With the guard only a late
restatement into an already tiered partition can trigger re-compaction of a Cool/Cold file and pay a fee for that one
file; accepted.

## Net result

Monthly net = tiering saving - tag cost - compaction read cost / 12 - steady-state crossing ops / 12 (one-off move not
amortised).

| Scale | Saving | Tags 5/part | Compaction reads | Crossing | Net (5 tags) | Net (2 tags, Proposal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 0.0048 | -0.0126 | -0.0010 | -0.0003 | **-0.0091** | **-0.0015** |
| M | 0.0286 | -0.0840 | -0.0060 | -0.0010 | **-0.0624** | **-0.0120** |
| L | 0.1431 | -0.1681 | -0.0242 | -0.0010 | **-0.0502** | **+0.0507** |

Plainly: with five tags per part and compaction/retag across all five datasets, tag-driven tiering is a **net loss at S, M
and L** at these volumes (a few cents per month; at L the tag charge alone is 117 % of the saving). If only `ticks`
existed (no bar datasets), the L tag cost would be 0.0412 EUR/month and the net positive (+0.10 EUR/month before reads);
that is not the planned system. The amounts are tiny in absolute terms; the point is that the "tiering saves about 55 %" in
T-121 holds for at-rest bytes only, not for the all-in cost at these volumes. T-503 should measure it.

## Proposal (for a separate ADR; `retag.py` unchanged)

Drop the path-derivable tags `dataset`, `instrument`, `data_year` (all recoverable from the blob path via
`layout.parse_md_path`), keep only `age_band` and `compacted`. Tags per part 5 -> 2 (-60 %):

| Scale | Tags | Tag cost EUR/month | Net EUR/month (table above) |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | 1,470 | 0.0050 | -0.0015 (about break-even) |
| M | 9,800 | 0.0336 | -0.0120 |
| L | 19,600 | 0.0672 | +0.0507 |

It turns L positive and shrinks the loss at S and M, but M stays slightly negative. Going to a single tag would need
`compacted` encoded elsewhere (a layout contract change); not proposed here.
